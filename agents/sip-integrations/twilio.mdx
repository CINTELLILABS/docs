Keep your numbers on a Twilio Elastic SIP Trunk and have a Bland agent answer them. Twilio sends inbound calls to Bland over TLS with SRTP, and Bland places outbound calls through your trunk's termination URI.

<Note>
**Two ways to use Twilio with Bland.** This guide covers **Elastic SIP Trunking**: your numbers stay on your trunk, Bland gets no Twilio API credentials, and you can split numbers between Bland and your own PBX. If you would rather give Bland API access to your Twilio account and let it run calls on your numbers directly, use [Custom Twilio](/tutorials/custom-twilio) instead. It needs no SIP setup.
</Note>

## What you'll need

- A Twilio account with Elastic SIP Trunking, and the numbers you want to use
- A Bland account with SIP trunking enabled. If you don't see a **SIP** tab under **Phone numbers**, contact Bland support to turn it on.

## Supported calling features

| Feature | Supported |
|---|---|
| Inbound calls to your agent | Yes |
| Outbound calls from your agent | Yes |
| Transfers from your agent | Yes, with SIP REFER |

## Configure Twilio

### Create the trunk

1. In the Twilio Console, go to **Elastic SIP Trunking**, then **Trunks**, and click **Create new SIP Trunk**. Give it a friendly name, for example `Bland`.
2. Under **General**, turn on **Secure Trunking**. Twilio then rejects any call that isn't encrypted, which matches Bland: TLS signaling and SRTP media.
3. Under **General**, turn on **Call Transfer (SIP REFER)** so your agent can transfer callers. To transfer to phone numbers, also turn on transfers to the PSTN.

### Route inbound calls to Bland

1. Under **Origination**, add an **Origination SIP URI** for your Bland region, with TLS on port `5061`:

   ```text
   sip:us2.sip.bland.ai:5061;transport=tls
   ```

   The other regions are under [Connection endpoints](/agents/sip-network#connection-endpoints). Use `sip:` with `;transport=tls`, as Twilio recommends, not `sips:`.

2. Under **Numbers**, click **Associate a Number with this Trunk** and add the numbers your agent should answer.

<Warning>
Use port `5061`. Bland reserves other ports on its SIP edge for its own carrier connections, and calls sent to them won't reach your agent.
</Warning>

Bland already trusts Twilio's published signaling IPs, so there is nothing to allow-list for inbound calls.

### Create termination credentials

Skip this section if you only need inbound calls.

1. Under **Termination**, note your **Termination SIP URI**, for example `yourcompany.pstn.twilio.com`. You enter it in Bland.
2. Under **Authentication**, create a **Credential List** with a username and password, and attach it to the trunk. You enter the same username and password in Bland.

Use credentials rather than an IP access control list. Bland's outbound calls do not come from a fixed set of IPs you can list.

## Configure Bland

1. At **All agents**, go to **Dispatch**, then **Phone numbers**, then the **SIP** tab, and click **Create trunk**.
2. Complete the [setup wizard](/agents/sip-setup):
   - **Provider**: choose **Twilio**, and enter your termination SIP URI, for example `yourcompany.pstn.twilio.com`. You don't need a port. Twilio is a cloud provider, so the wizard skips the firewall step.
   - **Authentication**: choose **IP-based**. Leave **Outbound routing** on **Via Bland's carrier**. Under **Outbound call credentials**, enter the username and password from your Twilio credential list. Then click **Create trunk**.
   - **Numbers**: add each number in E.164 format with a leading `+`, for example `+14155550100`.
3. Assign an agent to each number under **Phone numbers**, then **Configure**, then **Assignment**.

To place an outbound call from the API, use the number as `from` in [`POST /v1/calls`](/api-v1/post/calls).

## Caller ID

Twilio only accepts outbound calls whose caller ID is a number on your Twilio account or a verified caller ID, in E.164 format. Place outbound calls from a number on the trunk.

The same rule applies to transfers. A transfer that Bland bridges can't show the original caller's number to the target, because that number isn't on your Twilio account. With **Hand off via SIP REFER** on, Twilio places the transfer and can show the original caller: set **Caller ID for Transfer Target** to **Transferee** on the trunk (Twilio's default).

## Transfers

1. In the agent's **Transfer Call** node, turn on **Hand off via SIP REFER**.
2. Enter the target as a phone number in E.164 format. Bland sends a `REFER` to Twilio, Twilio calls the target, and Bland leaves the call.

Twilio can't transfer to emergency numbers, and early media isn't played during a REFER. For the transfer methods in detail, see [Transfers](/agents/sip-transfers).

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound calls don't reach Bland | Origination URI wrong, or Secure Trunking off | Use `sip:<region>.sip.bland.ai:5061;transport=tls` and turn on **Secure Trunking** |
| Call rejected with `603 Decline` | The number isn't attached in Bland | Attach the number on the Bland trunk in E.164 format |
| Outbound calls rejected with `401` or `403` | Credential mismatch | The username and password in Bland must match the credential list attached to the trunk |
| Outbound calls fail with `400` or a caller ID error | Caller ID isn't a Twilio number, or isn't E.164 | Call from a number on your Twilio account, with a leading `+` |
| Outbound calls dropped by Twilio | The request URI doesn't match your termination domain | Enter the termination SIP URI exactly as Twilio shows it |
| Transfers fail | Call Transfer is off on the trunk, or PSTN transfers aren't allowed | Turn on **Call Transfer (SIP REFER)**, and transfers to the PSTN |
| No audio | Secure Trunking off on one side | Turn on **Secure Trunking**. Twilio and Bland both use `AES_CM_128_HMAC_SHA1_80` |

For anything else, open the call's [SIP trace](/agents/sip-setup#inspect-a-call-sip-trace) on the trunk's **Overview** tab.
