A **trunk** is a reusable connection to your SIP infrastructure. It holds your provider's endpoint, the transport, and authentication. You create it once and attach numbers and DIDs to it.

<Note>
Using Bandwidth, Five9, NICE CXone, Twilio, Telnyx, or 3CX? Follow the matching [SIP integration guide](/agents/sip-integrations/overview). It covers the provider side and the exact wizard values.
</Note>

## Before you start

- An endpoint on your carrier, PBX, or SBC that Bland can reach over TLS on port 5061, with SRTP enabled
- Firewall access to allow-list Bland's [signaling and media IPs](/agents/sip-network#connection-endpoints)
- The numbers you will route, on your own carrier (numbers bought from Bland can't be attached to a trunk)

## Create a trunk with the wizard

On the **SIP** tab, click **Create trunk**. The wizard names the trunk from the provider and endpoint, and every trunk it builds carries both directions. Rename it later on the trunk's **Configure** tab. To create a one-direction or named trunk, use `POST /v1/sip/trunks`.

Each step below has an **API** section with the equivalent request.

<Warning>
The trunk is created at the **Authentication** step, not at the end. The button there reads **Create trunk**, and pressing it writes the trunk before you have picked a single number.

Leaving after that point leaves a real trunk behind with nothing attached to it.
</Warning>

### Step 1: Provider

Enter your SIP provider's endpoint address. The wizard includes a **provider catalog** with guided instructions for popular platforms:

| Provider | What to enter |
|---|---|
| [Twilio](/agents/sip-integrations/twilio) | Your Elastic SIP Trunk's termination SIP URI |
| Vonage | Your application's SIP endpoint |
| Asterisk / FreePBX | Your server's public IP or hostname |
| [3CX](/agents/sip-integrations/3cx) | Your 3CX system FQDN |
| Cisco UCM / CUBE | Your CUBE (SBC) address |
| RingCentral | A certified SBC in front of RingCentral |
| Microsoft Teams | Not supported directly. Connect through a certified SBC |
| **Other** | Any SIP endpoint, including [Bandwidth](/agents/sip-integrations/bandwidth) and [Telnyx](/agents/sip-integrations/telnyx) |

{/* IMAGE: the wizard Provider step, showing the provider catalog and the SIP server address input. Step 1 of 6 now, not 2. */}

The endpoint field accepts a hostname, an IP, a full SIP URI such as `sip:user@host:5061;transport=tls`, or shorthand such as `tls://host`. The wizard parses the host, port, and transport for you.

<Accordion title="API">
  Validate a destination before attaching with [`POST /v1/sip/parse-destination`](/api-v1/post/sip-parse-destination):

  ```bash
  curl -X POST https://api.bland.ai/v1/sip/parse-destination \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>" \
    -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
    -d '{ "input": "sip:your.provider.com:5061;transport=tls" }'
  ```

  Then set `sip_endpoint` on the trunk, or in the `directions` of your [`POST /v1/sip/attach`](/api-v1/post/sip-attach) request.
</Accordion>

### Step 2: Discovery

Bland probes your endpoint to find the right connection settings:

{/* IMAGE: the wizard Discovery step mid-probe, showing DNS resolution, port probing and the detected endpoint. Step 2 of 6. */}

1. **Resolves DNS:** A, AAAA, and SRV records (`_sip._udp`, `_sip._tcp`, `_sips._tcp`).
2. **Probes SIP ports:** sends SIP OPTIONS to common ports over UDP, TCP, and TLS.
3. **Analyzes responses:** detects your system (Asterisk, FreeSWITCH, Kamailio, 3CX, Cisco, and others), codecs, and methods.

It then shows the recommended port, transport, and system. You can also skip discovery and enter settings manually.

<Accordion title="API">
  Run discovery with [`POST /v1/sip/discover`](/api-v1/post/sip-discover). It runs the full probe and returns the result directly:

  ```bash
  curl -X POST https://api.bland.ai/v1/sip/discover \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>" \
    -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
    -d '{ "host": "sip.example.com" }'
  ```

  The `recommended` object in the response contains the optimal `port`, `transport`, and `detected_system`. One discovery can run per organization at a time; long-running sessions can also be polled with [`GET /v1/sip/discover/status`](/api-v1/get/sip-discover-status).
</Accordion>

### Step 3: Firewall

The wizard lists the Bland IPs and ports to allow-list on your firewall. The full reference is in [Connection endpoints](/agents/sip-network#connection-endpoints). Check the confirmation box to continue.

Cloud providers such as Twilio, Vonage, and RingCentral skip this step, because there is no firewall of yours to change. That is why some trunks have fewer steps.

<Accordion title="API">
  Retrieve your region's IPs and ports programmatically with [`GET /v1/sip/firewall-ips`](/api-v1/get/sip-firewall-ips):

  ```bash
  curl -X GET https://api.bland.ai/v1/sip/firewall-ips \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>"
  ```
</Accordion>

### Step 4: Authentication

Choose how the two sides authenticate.

{/* IMAGE: the wizard Authentication step, showing the IP-based and registration options and the Create trunk button. */}

- **IP-based (recommended):** your server trusts Bland's signaling and media IPs as SIP peers, and Bland trusts your endpoint by source IP. No credentials to manage. Transfers and warm transfer consult calls that Bland sends to your server connect from the media IPs, so trust all of the IPs listed under [Connection endpoints](/agents/sip-network#connection-endpoints).
- **Registration-based:** Bland registers with your server using a username and password via SIP REGISTER. A derived password is returned that you configure in your PBX.

Then choose how outbound calls leave Bland:

- **Outbound routing**
  - **Via Bland's carrier (default):** Bland's carrier places the call to your SIP endpoint. Nothing else to set up.
  - **Direct to your carrier (BYOC):** Bland sends the call straight to your carrier, so termination runs on your own carrier contract. Your carrier must support TLS, and the caller IDs you dial from must be authorized on that account.
- **Outbound call credentials:** the SIP username and password your carrier gave you. Bland uses them to answer a `401` or `407` challenge on outbound calls. Optional with Bland's carrier, required with direct routing.

<Accordion title="API">
  Set `auth_mode` on the trunk or in the inbound direction of [`POST /v1/sip/attach`](/api-v1/post/sip-attach):

  ```json
  {
    "type": "inbound",
    "auth_mode": "register",
    "register_auth": {
      "username": "my-trunk",
      "password": "a-strong-password"
    }
  }
  ```

  When REGISTER auth is set, the response includes `register_password_for_pbx`: a one-time derived password to configure in your PBX.

  Need a password to start from? [`GET /v1/sip/generate-password`](/api-v1/get/sip-generate-password) returns a strong, PBX-safe one at a configurable length (`length` 8 to 256, or `strength` `standard`/`long`).
</Accordion>

### Step 5: Numbers

Add the numbers to route through the trunk, or skip and add them later from the trunk's **Numbers** tab. Numbers bought from Bland can't be attached to a trunk.

### Step 6: Complete

The trunk is live. Each trunk page has three tabs: **Overview** (call volume, answer rate, and call logs), **Numbers**, and **Configure** (trunk, authentication, headers, and firewall). **Test connection** in the top bar sends a SIP OPTIONS ping to your endpoint over TLS.

<Accordion title="API">
  Confirm the trunk and its bound numbers with [`GET /v1/sip/trunks/:id`](/agents/sip#api), and check per-number health with [`GET /v1/sip/status`](/api-v1/get/sip-status):

  ```bash
  curl -X GET 'https://api.bland.ai/v1/sip/status?phone_number=%2B14155550100' \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>"
  ```
</Accordion>

## Attach numbers and DIDs

Numbers inherit the trunk's endpoint, transport, and authentication, so you don't repeat them per number. Bind numbers to a trunk with [`POST /v1/sip/attach`](/api-v1/post/sip-attach). You can attach up to **100** at once.

- **PSTN numbers** are normalized to E.164, for example `+14155550100`.
- **DIDs and extensions**: when a number is bound to a trunk (`trunk_id`), non-E.164 SIP identities such as DIDs and dial-plan extensions (for example `2001`) are accepted and stored as-is.
- Each number can carry an **inbound** and an **outbound** configuration independently.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.bland.ai/v1/sip/attach \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "phone_numbers": ["+14155550100", "2001"],
    "directions": [{ "type": "inbound" }, { "type": "outbound" }],
    "service": "sip",
    "trunk_id": "<trunk-id>"
  }'
```

The response lists `configured` numbers and `failed` numbers with a reason for each.

## Inbound numbers

Inbound SIP numbers behave like any other Bland inbound number. Assign an agent and configure them under **Phone numbers** as usual.

## Inspect a call (SIP trace)

Open a trunk's **Overview** tab and select a call to see its **SIP trace**: every message on every leg, as a sortable table or a **ladder (PCAP)** diagram.

{/* IMAGE: a SIP trace on the trunk Overview tab, showing the INVITE / 100 Trying / 180 Ringing flow with headers. */}

The trace includes full headers and SDP, including transfer legs. Copy or export it to share with your carrier. The call log beside it refreshes on its own and shows direction, status, SIP response code, and duration.

## Advanced configuration

[`PATCH /v1/sip/config`](/api-v1/patch/sip-config) saves failover servers, codec preferences, and alert thresholds for a number. Bland stores these settings but does not apply them to calls yet, and there is no dashboard control for them:

- Calls use the single endpoint on the trunk. There is no automatic failover to a secondary server.
- Calls use Bland's standard codec list (see [Requirements and security](/agents/sip-network#requirements-and-security)), whatever order you save.
- No SIP-specific alerts are sent.
